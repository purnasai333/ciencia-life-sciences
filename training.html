Chemistry:
Chemistry, the study of matter and its structure, composition, and properties. Industrial difficulties are inextricably linked to the more abstract problems in chemistry

The scope of Chemistry:
One can no longer expect to have a complete understanding of all of chemistry. Those interested in specialised branches of chemistry are able to network with people who share their passions. Over time, a subfield of chemistry will form around a core group of researchers who share a common interest. Organic, inorganic, physical, analytical, and industrial chemistry, and biochemistry, which all emerged early in the history of chemistry, continue to attract the most attention.

Analytical Chemistry:
All other branches of chemistry benefit from the ability to isolate individual chemicals and analyse complex combinations. Never before has analytical chemistry been as crucial as it is now. Analytical chemistry methods are often used for ecological upkeep. Chemists' ability to accurately identify chemicals has been bolstered by the development of increasingly sophisticated analytic tools, many of which are now connected with computers to further enhance accuracy

Our team's main concerns include methodical planning and Assay method development and HPLC validation, as well as method development and HPLC validation for related chemicals, are just two examples of the many analytical test parameters validated by HPLC.

We provide in-depth training in all aspects of method validation, including method transfer and stability studies in accordance with ICH guidelines.

We also discuss the creation and validation of UV spectrophotometric methods for determining API concentrations in pharmaceutical formulations.

Molecular biology is a branch of science concerning biological activity at the molecular level. Molecular biology looks at the molecular mechanisms behind processes such as replication, transcription, translation and cell function. One way to describe the basis of molecular biology is to say it concerns understanding how genes are transcribed into RNA and how RNA is then translated into protein.

Biomarkers (short for biological markers) are biological measures of a biological state. The use of biomarkers in basic and clinical research as well as in clinical practice has become so common place that their presence as primary endpoints in clinical trials is now accepted almost without question. In the case of specific biomarkers that have been well characterized and repeatedly shown to correctly predict relevant clinical outcomes across a variety of treatments and populations. This training will give the current conceptual status of biomarkers as clinical and how to develop biomarker as diagnostic tool.

R is a programming language for statistics and data science developed by Ross Ihaka and Robert Gentleman in 1993

R is an open source scripting language for predictive analytics and data visualization.

R language is a platform independent so we can use it on any operating systems. It allows you to integrate with other languages (C, C++).

R is a leading tool for machine learning, statistics, and data analysis and graphical representation etc. Data Scientists, Statisticians, Analysts and others who need to make sense of data use R for statistical analysis, data visualization, and predictive modelling.

R is open source community. We can create contributed additional functionality to the R language by creating add-on "packages" for use by the 2 million users of R worldwide. (www.r-bloggers.com)

R products: RStudio is a free, open source IDE (integrated development environment) for R. It makes R easier to use. It includes a code editor, debugging, & visualization tools.

Who uses R: Telecom, Pharmaceuticals, Financial Services, Life Sciences, Education etc.

R is free to download as it is licensed under the terms of GNU General Public license. IEEE publishes R was 5th popular programming language in 2016. According to O’Reilly Media, 2014 survey on Data Science Salary learning R will increase chances of getting a job.
