2. Organisation and Approach

The About page says Ciencia was started by PhD holders with research backgrounds who observed demand for an integrated service provider for fast-growing life-sciences and biotechnology companies and academics. Its stated motto is to provide research training from qualified professionals alongside integrated consulting and research outsourcing.

The site describes Ciencia as a bioinformatics training organisation in Hyderabad. It lists advanced training, short courses, summer projects, and Bio-IT courses such as BioPerl and Python in classroom and online formats. Research support includes project data analysis and help preparing projects for funding agencies and publications. Clinical-trial and pharmaceutical research support is described as using cheminformatics tools.

Stated working principles

  • Qualified professionals and doctoral faculty are emphasized in training and research guidance.
  • Hands-on instrument experience and practical exposure to protocols are emphasized for students.
  • Data-analysis and research-support teams are described as assisting throughout project stages, with outputs and progress reporting mentioned on the Research Services page.
  • The organisation states that client information and research work are kept confidential and not shared with other persons or companies.
  • Training and project curricula may be adjusted to a candidate’s eligibility, according to the course pages.

Confidentiality, quality, timeline, and outcome statements above are descriptions made by the organisation on its website; service terms should be agreed in writing for each engagement.

7. Bioinformatics and Cheminformatics

Ciencia lists training, projects, and research support in biological databases, genomics, proteomics, sequence analysis, molecular modelling, ligand screening, docking, ADMET prediction, and computer-aided drug design (CADD). The site promotes classroom and online formats and practical exposure using tools and databases.

Bioinformatics topics listed

  • Biological data, data retrieval, and biological databases: nucleotide, protein-sequence, domain, and structure resources; examples include GenBank, EMBL, DDBJ, UniProt, PDB, NDB, MMDB, SCOP, CATH, KEGG, and related resources.
  • Sequence alignment and database searching: pairwise and multiple alignment; local/global alignment; BLAST, FASTA, PSI-BLAST, and ClustalW; scoring matrices such as PAM and BLOSUM.
  • Gene prediction, functional analysis, ORF finding, primer design, ESTs, gene discovery, and restriction-site detection.
  • DNA/RNA secondary-structure prediction; protein homology modelling, threading, structure/function sites, binding-site analysis, and model validation.
  • Phylogenetics, genetic algorithms, computational biology algorithms, genomics, proteomics, and sequence/structure interpretation.
  • Programming and computational foundations appearing in the syllabus: C, C++, Python, Biopython, BioPerl, R, Oracle, Unix/Linux.

Cheminformatics, molecular modelling, and CADD topics listed

  • Chemical structure formats, sketching, viewing, and conversion; examples listed include ChemDraw, ChemSketch, Marvin, Ketcher, Chimera, PyMOL, OpenBabel, and other viewers.
  • Compound and biological databases; small-molecule library preparation; compound properties and drug-likeness, including Lipinski-rule concepts.
  • Molecular mechanics and quantum chemistry concepts: force fields, energy expressions and minimisation, local/global minima, molecular geometry, conformational search, semi-empirical methods, and molecular dynamics.
  • Protein target preparation, sequence/structure analysis, homology modelling, threading, active/binding-site analysis, and model refinement.
  • Molecular docking, scoring, interaction analysis, fragment-based docking, and selection of compounds for biological screening.
  • Pharmacophore generation (structure- and ligand-based), similarity/substructure screening, virtual screening, and virtual-library design.
  • ADME/ADMET prediction, QSAR and quantitative structure–property relationships, molecular descriptors, model generation and validation, SAR, and drug-discovery workflows.
  • The syllabus mentions AI and machine-learning applications in drug discovery/design.

Immunoinformatics topics listed

  • Immunological databases, including MHC, T-cell epitope, and B-cell epitope resources.
  • Antibody homology modelling.
  • Prediction of MHC class I cytotoxic T-cell epitopes and MHC class II helper T-cell epitopes.
  • B-cell epitope prediction, antigen recognition, and computational vaccine-design resources.
  • In-silico immunogenicity work: T-cell epitope prediction from primary sequences, MHC peptide-binding prediction, linear versus conformational B-cell epitope prediction, and antigen structure prediction via homology modelling.

Product snapshot elements listed

Chemical name; structure; CAS number; dosage form and strength; approval information in the US, Europe (EP), and India (IN); DMF filer list; CEP filer list; Orange Book patents; polymorphic information; marketed versus targeted form; possible prior-art route-of-synthesis suggestions; and an IP conclusion.

How selected reports are described

  • Landscape: searches across databases using product and IUPAC-name keywords; identifies relevant global patents/applications, disclosed synthetic schemes, and legal status. The prose says a minimum of about 20–25 days; the table says 20–30 days.
  • IP clearance: examines a customer-provided route for potential infringement in a specified geography and describes a country-specific non-infringement opinion.
  • FTO/infringement: claim-based analysis of valid patents/applications in a particular country, generally described as work before a product launch.
  • Patentability: assesses novelty, inventive step, and industrial applicability as described on the page; obtain professional advice for the applicable jurisdiction and facts.
